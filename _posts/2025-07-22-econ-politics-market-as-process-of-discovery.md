It is common to talk about the economy as if it were a mechanical engine, something governments can speed up or slow down by simply adjusting a few levers of monetary policy. However, thinkers from the Austrian School remind us that capital is not a homogeneous block of wealth. It is an intricate, ever-shifting network of human plans.

General equilibrium models, those elegant mathematical graphs with their perfect curves of supply and demand, have a massive blind spot. They assume that everyone has perfect information and they completely ignore the factor of time. The real market is never actually in equilibrium. It is a continuous process of discovery where millions of people are desperately trying to coordinate their future expectations based on highly fragmented knowledge.

## Questions that arise from thinking about this topic

- If economic value is purely subjective, changing every second in the mind of every individual, is it even theoretically possible for a centralized entity to calculate the correct price needed to coordinate an entire society?
