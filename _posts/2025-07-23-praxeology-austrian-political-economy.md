**Praxeology**

Science of human action as such. Founded by Ludwig von Mises in _Human Action_ (1949). Basic principle: all human action is purposeful (seeks to move from a less satisfactory to a more satisfactory state), occurs in time and under uncertainty, and uses scarce means to achieve ends. Praxeological truths are derived a priori from the nature of action, not from statistical observation.

_Related:_ methodological individualism, human action, apriorism, teleology
_Cross-references:_ [Agent-Based Modeling (ABM)](/blog/2024/agent-based-modeling/), [Social Sciences / Behavioral Economics](/blog/2024/social-sciences-behavioral-economics/)
_Key work:_ Mises, L. von (1949). _Human Action: A Treatise on Economics_. Yale University Press.

**Ordinal value**

Value as an ordering of preferences, not as a measurable cardinal magnitude. "I prefer A to B" is a valid and verifiable praxeological proposition. "A is worth twice as much as B" is not, because value has no objective cardinal scale. Consequence: the valuations of different individuals cannot be summed or aggregated. This is the basis of both Mises' calculation argument and Arrow's impossibility theorem.

_Related:_ subjective value, preferences, ordinal utility, cardinality
_Cross-references:_ [Social Sciences / Behavioral Economics](/blog/2024/social-sciences-behavioral-economics/)
_Key work:_ Mises, L. von (1920/1935). Economic calculation in the socialist commonwealth. In F. A. Hayek (Ed.), _Collectivist Economic Planning_. Routledge.

**The economic calculation problem** (Mises, 1920)

Logical impossibility of rational economic calculation without market prices based on private property. Not a problem of information (which could in principle be solved with more data) or computational capacity (which could be solved with more processing). A problem about the nature of value: subjective, ordinal, and revealed only through voluntary exchange under real scarcity. Eliminate the exchanges and the information disappears.

_Related:_ price, private property, coordination, dispersed knowledge
_Cross-references:_ [Artificial Intelligence / Machine Learning](/blog/2025/ai-ml/), [Complex Systems / Network Science](/blog/2022/complex-systems-network-science/), [Information Theory / Science and Technology](/blog/2026/information-theory-sci-tech/)
_Key work:_ Mises, L. von (1920/1935). Economic calculation in the socialist commonwealth. In F. A. Hayek (Ed.), _Collectivist Economic Planning_. Routledge.

**Tacit knowledge** (Hayek, 1945)

Knowledge that cannot be explicitly articulated or centrally transmitted: "the knowledge of the particular circumstances of time and place." Example: an entrepreneur knows that their usual supplier will be slower this week; that information is not in any database. Prices are the mechanism that aggregates and transmits this knowledge without requiring it in explicit form.

_Related:_ dispersed knowledge, price signals, epistemology, local knowledge
_Cross-references:_ [Information Theory / Science and Technology](/blog/2026/information-theory-sci-tech/)
_Key work:_ Hayek, F. A. (1945). The use of knowledge in society. _American Economic Review_, 35(4), 519–530.

**Arrow's impossibility theorem** (1951)

There is no procedure for aggregating individual ordinal preference rankings into a collective social preference that simultaneously satisfies five minimum fairness conditions: unrestricted domain, unanimity (Pareto), independence of irrelevant alternatives, non-dictatorship, and transitivity. Not a result about computational limits: a theorem about the logical structure of ordinal preference aggregation. Converges with Mises' calculation argument from the mathematics of social choice theory.

_Related:_ social welfare, social choice, welfare function, Condorcet paradox
_Cross-references:_ [Artificial Intelligence / Machine Learning](/blog/2025/ai-ml/), [Social Sciences / Behavioral Economics](/blog/2024/social-sciences-behavioral-economics/)
_Key work:_ Arrow, K. J. (1951). _Social Choice and Individual Values_. Wiley.

**Catallactics / Catallaxy** (Hayek)

Term Hayek preferred to "economics" to describe the order that emerges from voluntary market interaction among many individuals. From the Greek katalattein: to exchange, but also to admit into the community, to turn an enemy into a friend. Emphasis: the market does not allocate resources toward predetermined ends; it generates outcomes nobody anticipated and nobody could have planned.

_Related:_ spontaneous order, cosmos, market process, competition
_Cross-references:_ [Complex Systems / Network Science](/blog/2022/complex-systems-network-science/), [Systems Engineering](/blog/2023/systems-engineering/)
_Key work:_ Hayek, F. A. (1976). _Law, Legislation and Liberty, Vol. 2: The Mirage of Social Justice_. University of Chicago Press.
