Software engineering used to be a lot like clockmaking. You wrote the code, carefully adjusted the gears, tested it, and if one piece broke, the whole system ground to a halt. With the rise of artificial intelligence and agent-oriented architectures, we are shifting from building clocks to cultivating gardens.

We are no longer defining every single line of execution. Instead, we are building autonomous nodes that interact with each other, deciding independently how to solve a problem. The mathematical abstraction of engineering is moving away from deterministic functions where if A happens, the result is B, toward probabilistic systems.

## Questions that arise from thinking about this topic

- If modern software is designed to update, test, and even rewrite itself at the edge of the network, what becomes of the human engineer?
- Are we transitioning from being the builders of bridges to becoming the legislators of rules?
