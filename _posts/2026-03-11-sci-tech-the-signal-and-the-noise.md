Our current era is largely defined by an obsession with accumulating data, driven by the false illusion that having more data inevitably equals having more knowledge. Information theory, however, defines real information as that which successfully reduces our uncertainty.

Mathematically, this is measured through the concept of entropy. As we hyper-connect sensors, devices, and servers, the sheer volume of noise in the system grows exponentially. Our digital infrastructure becomes vast, but also incredibly fragile.

Cybersecurity and network engineering are no longer just about building an impenetrable wall of code. They operate on the assumption that the wall will eventually fall. The true scientific challenge is not figuring out how to keep the noise out permanently, but rather how to design networks with true digital resilience.

## Questions that arise from thinking about this topic

- How do we build technological architectures that do not just survive failure, but are capable of degrading gracefully and recovering autonomously?
