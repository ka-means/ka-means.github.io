A biological virus, a fake news story, and a bank run all spread following the same fundamental laws. To truly understand these phenomena, we have to stop looking at the individuals involved and start looking at the invisible structure that binds them together.

In the mathematics of networks, people or institutions are nodes, and their interactions are links. The speed at which an idea travels often has less to do with the quality of the idea itself and more to do with the topological position of the person sharing it. Diffusion models explain why a behavior can lie dormant for months and then suddenly go viral when it crosses a critical threshold of connectivity.

When we look at how information flows today, profound structural questions arise.

## Questions that arise from thinking about this topic

- Is it even possible to stop a systemic contagion when the very architecture of our global networks is mathematically designed to eliminate all friction?
